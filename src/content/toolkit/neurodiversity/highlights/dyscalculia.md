### In short

Dyscalculia makes numbers harder to understand and work with, including time, codes and deadlines. It has nothing to do with intelligence. Small changes help, such as spelling out numbers in full, and letting people use calculators freely.

### Overview

Dyscalculia is a neurological learning difference that affects how someone understands and processes numbers. It goes well beyond disliking maths.

It can affect things like estimating time, remembering numerical codes and passwords, reading graphs, keeping track of deadlines, or even working out how long it will take to get somewhere.

Around 5 to 6% of the population are thought to have it, yet research into dyscalculia is estimated to be about 30 years behind research into dyslexia, despite similar prevalence. That means many people go unidentified for years, often assuming they are simply “bad at maths.”

At work, dyscalculia can quietly affect budgeting tasks, scheduling, interpreting data, and time management. It has nothing to do with intelligence.

Someone with dyscalculia might be a brilliant communicator, creative thinker, or strategic problem-solver, but still struggle to recall a PIN under pressure or estimate how long a project will take. Without awareness, these challenges can be misread as carelessness or lack of focus.

The good news is that small changes make a real difference. Spelling out numbers in full, providing templates with time expectations, sharing visual reference guides, and giving people space to use calculators freely are all low-effort adjustments with a big impact. You do not need a formal diagnosis to ask for support, or to offer it.
